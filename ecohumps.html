ECOHUMPS
Biomimetic Food Storage System
Inspired by the moisture-regulating properties of dromedary camel fur, this project explores a biodegradable multi-layer food container designed to reduce moisture buildup and extend food freshness.

NATURE'S STRATEGY FOR MOISTURE REGULATION
Camels regulate heat and moisture through layered insulation that controls exchange rather than trapping it.
• Dense fur insulation blocks external heat transfer from the hot environment to the skin
• Maintains up to 30 °C temperature gradient between surface and skin
• Minimizes need for evaporative cooling (sweating)
• Conserves water through reduced sweating
Camel hair microstructure enhances insulation at a microscopic scale.
• Three-layer fiber architecture - cuticle, cortex, medulla
• Air-filled core traps heat
• Enables insulation at micro scale
BIOMIMETIC PRODUCT MAPPING
Outer Protective Layer
Shields from external heat and humidity
Food-Safe Inner Layer
Maintains hygiene and freshness
Moisture-Regulating Layer
Buffers internal moisture exchange
Layered, breathable systems regulate moisture more effectively than airtight sealing.
MATERIAL CANDIDATES
Chitosan
• Natural biopolymer derived from chitin
• Naturally breathable
Suitable for moisture regulation
Polylactic Acid (PLA)
• Bio-based thermoplastic derived from renewable sources
Structural stability and Strong moisture barrier
Compostable alternative to plastic
Polyhydroxybutyrate-co-hydroxyvalerate (PHBV)
• Biodegradable polyester produced through microbial fermentation
Good flexibility
Potential composite layer
MATERIAL TESTING
Biopolymer films were fabricated using solution casting and ultrasonic blending in the chemistry lab to evaluate their suitability for moisture-resistant food storage.
Experiments evaluated:
• Film formation and structural stability
• Moisture permeability using WVTR testing
• Material compatibility in composite systems
• Mechanical behavior and flexibility
Films fabricated during material testing
Pure PLA
Pure chitosan
Chitosan + PHBV
Chitosan + PLA
Material testing revealed distinct differences in moisture permeability between the films.
• PLA: strongest moisture barrier (lowest WVTR)
•Chitosan: moderate permeability enabling moisture regulation
•Chitosan + PHBV and Chitosan + PLA : higher permeability due to polymer incompatibility
Rather than blending materials, functional separation through layering proved more effective for moisture regulation.
This insight informed the development of a multi-layer container architecture, where different materials perform specific roles within the system.
Full material testing methodology and analysis available in the research paper.
