Mapping the Systems Behind Volunteer Engagement in NGO Schools
An exploration of volunteer recruitment and retention challenges within educational NGOs. Through field research, interviews and systems-thinking techniques like causal mapping, the Iceberg Model and Five Whys, I uncovered underlying issues and opportunities to build a more sustainable volunteer ecosystem.
My Focus
· Field Research & Data Analysis · System-Level Ideation · UX Research & Interface Design
Duration — 2 week | Year — 2024
Tools used
Educational NGOs
play an important role in creating access to learning and opportunities for children from underserved communities. However, their functioning extends far beyond classrooms, involving a network of students, educators, volunteers, donors, resources and community stakeholders.
Visited seven educational NGOs across Pune, along with private and government schools, to compare learning environments, teaching approaches and infrastructure. The NGO visits further helped to understand their educational practices, operational structures and community engagement.
Key Field Observations
Diverse Approaches to Education
Formal schooling, bridge education, informal learning and skill-based programmes.
Learning Beyond Academics
Mentorship, creative activities, life skills and socio-emotional development.
Uneven Resources & Support Systems
Differences in infrastructure, funding, partnerships and operational capacity.
Volunteer Roles & Continuity
Different levels of volunteer involvement, training and long-term participation.
Research methods
Field visits · Direct observation · Interactions · Visual documentation
Mapped the broader NGO ecosystem to understand the different elements that influence how these organisations function.
LOOKING BENEATH THE SURFACE
Applying the Iceberg Model
After mapping the larger NGO ecosystem, we recognised that several challenges were interconnected and could not be understood through visible incidents alone.
What the framework helped me investigate
Visible events Recurring patterns System structures Mental models
Events - Students dropping out from educational institutions
Patterns | What keeps happening?
Student dropouts between primary and secondary education, irregular attendance and gaps in learning.
Structures | What is causing these patterns?
Language barriers, difficulty adapting to academic expectations, peer influence and declining motivation.
Mental Models | What beliefs influence the system?
Education is not necessary for girls". "He wont learn anyways, why waste money". "My child needs to earn not read books". "Children from poor background want succeed anyway". "English is the only way to succeed" Schooling is only made rich
Events - Ongoing efforts to recruit volunteers
Patterns | What keeps happening?
Seasonal recruitment drives, social media outreach and college partnerships. High initial participation then volunteer shortages and declining engagement.
Structures | What is causing these patterns?
Over reliance, non structured, no follow ups, inadequate training, non formalized, unrealistic expectation, lack of female, internal commitment, external motivation, paid support, no family or peer support, personal commitment
Mental Models | What beliefs influence the system?
"Volunteers are temporary dont expect much". "Volunteers = extra help not essential work". "Volunteers is equal to free labor". "Good people volunteer and good people say no". "Someone else will handle it".
Events - Students are motivated to go for NGO Sessions
Patterns | What keeps happening?
Increase in attendence, social intraction skills, one to one volunteer interaction , children feel emotionly and physically safer, increase in recognition of students
Structures | What is causing these patterns?
Easy to join, no uniform , exposure to co-curricular activity , value education, community involvement, celebrating small achievement, non traditional evaluation method
Mental Models | What beliefs influence the system?
"Traditional school are rigid and not engaging". "Children can learn better if taught with care and empathy" "i learn more here than regular school" "Education can help me change my future". "we are not judged & punished here" "i can ask question without fear"
CONNECTING THE DOTS
The Iceberg analysis helped to identify deeper issues within the NGO ecosystem. Volunteer engagement emerged as an area worth exploring further.
Mapped the relationships between recruitment, motivation, training, satisfaction and retention to understand how one factor could influence another.
Leverage point - By addressing volunteer interest degradation, we can begin to resolve deeper issues such as leadership gaps, weak infrastructure, limited growth opportunities, and social disconnection. This, in turn, strengthens the overall effectiveness of the NGO.

5 why analysis
Frequent drop-offs after initial participation
Why do they participate short term?
They volunteer in their free time.
Why only in free time?
Academic schedules limit availability.
Why does limited availability lead to drop-offs?
Inflexible schedules disrupt continuity.
Why is continuity difficult to maintain?
Lack of regular follow-ups and engagement.
Why are volunteers not consistently re-engaged?
Limited communication, recognition and relationship-building.
Reluctance to travel long distances for volunteering
Why do volunteers face challenges?
Long travel and scheduling conflicts.
Why are travel and scheduling an issue?
Limited time, money and public transport dependency.
Why are these constraints difficult to overcome?
NGO locations and timings mismatch volunteer availability.
Why is volunteer allocation inflexible?
Fixed school schedules and limited flexibility.
Why is flexibility not considered?
Travel constraints and availability overlooked during planning.
Training overload and skill mismatch
Why do volunteers lack the required skills?
Diverse backgrounds and limited experience.
Why is their experience not identified?
No skill assessment before role assignment.
Why are unsuitable roles assigned?
Recruitment prioritises numbers over skill alignment.
Why is skill alignment not addressed through training?
Training is generic, not role-specific.
Why is training not role-specific?
Limited resources and no structured programmes.

Why does excitement fade?
activities stop feeling engaging.
Why not engaging?
tasks become repetitive and mismatched.
Why mismatched?
volunteer interests aren't understood well.
Why not understood?
poor onboarding and communication.
Why poor onboarding?
ngos lack resources for volunteer management.
PROBLEM STATEMENT
Educational NGOs depend on committed volunteers to create lasting social impact, yet volunteering often remains a short-term, inconsistent experience as people struggle to find opportunities that align with their interests, skills and availability, while NGOs' limited flexibility, structured guidance and recognition make sustained participation difficult.
How might we increase long term commitment of volunteers for NGO’s so that social and emotional impact strengthens?

WHAT CAN MAKE VOLUNTEERING SUSTAINABLE?
What we learned
Across the volunteer models studied, a few recurring factors stood out:
01. Structured Engagement
Clear roles and organised onboarding.
02. Meaningful Participation
Opportunities to contribute to a cause.
03. Personal Growth
Skill development and practical experience.
04. Continued Connection
Recognition, community and ongoing engagement.
The Gap
While existing models offer effective ways to engage volunteers, discovering relevant opportunities, finding the right fit and maintaining involvement can still be challenging.
